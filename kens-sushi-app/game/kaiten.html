How to play
The goal
Serve every customer by making the plate they ordered. Matching every pair is not the goal: you don't need to clear the board, and plates nobody ordered are wasted. The service ends when the last customer leaves, or when the board runs out of fish. Customers still to come then go home, so every pair you waste is a customer you can’t feed. Three stars needs every customer fed.
Making plates
Tap two free tiles with the same fish. A tile is free when nothing sits on top of it and its left or right side is open.
Serving
Customers take the plate they ordered as it passes their seat, and happy customers pay more. Tap an order to see where that fish is on the board.
Order streak
Make plates only for orders and your streak grows: each plate in a row pays 10% more, up to 50%. A plate that goes on the belt for nobody, or a walk-out, ends the streak. A prepped plate joins the streak when someone orders it.
Prep counter
Make a pair nobody has ordered yet and Ken keeps it on the prep counter beside him. Two plates fit there, and they stay fresh. When someone orders one, it goes straight onto the belt. Customers often ask for what’s ready. It’s most useful while you dig toward a buried order: keep a space free for that.
Station improvements
Everything you earn also goes into the till. Spend it on a faster belt, a sharper knife (customers tip into the till) and better seating, or on lanterns, a noren and Katsu. Each has five levels, and every level changes how it looks. The top prize is Katsu’s Cushion. Spending never lowers your takings or league place.
Weekly league
Your takings each week put you in a league with up to 30 players: Iwashi, Saba, Tai, Buri, Maguro and Ōtoro. Finish in the top 20% to move up; the bottom 20% drop down. Weeks end on Sunday at midnight UTC.
Ranks
Higher ranks pay more: Wakiita ×1.5, Itamae ×2 and Taishō ×3, plus a little more with every level. Past level 30, every Taishō level is a master level: customers are a little less patient, more of them come, rushes come sooner, and the pay keeps rising.
Walk-outs
A customer who runs out of patience walks out and leaves a one-star review. It costs half the price of the plate they wanted.
Waste
When the prep counter is full, plates nobody ordered go on the belt. They earn nothing, and after about two laps they dry out, Ken bins them, and you pay for the fish. Plates still on the belt or the prep counter when the service ends are waste too.
Omakase
When the counter is calm, a guest may order omakase and leave the choice to Ken. He picks three fish you can reach, and only when there’s fish to spare. Make the three plates before the time runs out: the guest pays one and a half times their price, and you get a free wasabi. Ken takes those plates himself, so they never go to waste. If a customer is waiting for the same fish, the plate goes to the customer first, so make another for the omakase.
Music
“Ken’s cozy counter” is played on real instruments recorded by Versilian Studios and shared free for anyone to use (CC0). The speaker button switches between sound and music, sound effects only, and silence.
Seasonal fish
About every three weeks a new seasonal fish comes in, on every board. Serve one while it’s in season and it joins your fish book for good; miss it and you’ll have to wait for it to come round again. Today’s catch shows when the next one arrives, but not which fish it is.
Today’s catch
Every day Captain Kasumi brings in one fish that is in season. It is on every board that day, its tiles and plates carry a red 旬 tag, and it sells for more. Serve one to stamp it in your fish book.
Hint
Ken marks a free pair that serves a waiting customer. If none is free, he marks the pair that digs toward one. The lantern glows when you seem stuck. Out of hints? A short ad lights it.
Wasabi
Customers don’t change their order, so when someone’s fish is stuck under the stack, Ken will point you to wasabi. It serves a customer whose fish is buried first, or else the one who has waited longest: Ken pulls their fish up from under the stack, the pair you can’t reach yet, and hands the plate straight over, so the tiles you can reach stay for the next order. When a customer is about to leave and the tea is gone, a wasabi leaf appears over them: tap it to serve them. Out of wasabi? A short ad gets you one.
Tea
Ken's teapot holds 2 cups a service, 3 for Ken's Club. A cup tops up one customer's patience by half, once per customer. When someone is running out of patience, a teacup appears over them: tap it to pour. The first cup each service is on the house; after that each cup is a short ad unless you're in the Club.
Lunch rush
From the second rank on, rushes come in waves. Plates pay more and patience runs short.
Pause
Tap the timer. The board and the orders stay hidden until you resume.
